1
Preheat oven to 350°F and butter a 9×13 baking dish.
2
Shred all 2.5 lbs of block cheese (16 oz mozzarella, 16 oz Colby Jack, 8 oz sharp cheddar). Toss together and split into 2 equal bowls (one for the sauce, one for layering).
3
Boil 1 lb cavatappi in heavily salted water for 8 min (or 7 min if baking later). Drain well.
4
Make roux: Melt 3 tbsp butter over medium heat. Bloom half the seasoning blend for 30 sec, whisk in 3 tbsp flour, and cook 2–3 min.
5
Build sauce: Slowly stream in 1 can (12 oz) evaporated milk, whisking 1 min until smooth. Whisk in 1 tbsp Dijon mustard, then 2 cups (1 pint) heavy cream and the remaining seasoning blend. Simmer ~2 min.
6
Turn heat to LOW. Melt in Bowl #1 of shredded cheese one handful at a time until smooth. Fold in the cooked cavatappi.
7
Layer & bake: Half mac → half of Bowl #2 cheese → remaining mac → rest of cheese on top. Bake 25–30 min at 350°F, then broil 2 min to brown. Garnish with chopped chives.

The Viral Dish

In November 2023, chef Tineke "Tini" Younger posted her baked macaroni and cheese recipe on TikTok, where it racked up over 100 million views and sparked a massive surge in her following.

Built around cavatappi pasta and 2.5 pounds of freshly grated mozzarella, Colby Jack, and sharp cheddar, the recipe became so popular for Thanksgiving that Younger is partly credited with causing a nationwide cavatappi pasta shortage during the holidays.

About the Creator: Tineke "Tini" Younger

Tineke "Tini" Younger (pronounced TEE-nə-kə YUNG-ər) is an American chef, cookbook author, and social media personality born and raised in Walkersville, Maryland.

  • Training: Learned to cook through culinary programs at Walkersville High School and Frederick Community College.

  • Cookbooks: Published Cooking for my Boyfriend (2023) and released Today We Are Cooking, Recipes That Teach You to Think Like a Chef on November 3, 2026, which immediately became a #1 New York Times bestseller.
  • Recognition: Named to Forbes 30 Under 30 in Food and Drink in 2026, with roughly 16 million combined social media followers.

Why Cavatappi Pasta?

Cavatappi (Italian for "corkscrews", literally "stopper extractor") is a hollow, ridged (rigati) macaroni tube extruded into a 1-to-3-turn helix. It is also sold as Cellentani, spirali, curly mac, or "double elbows."

Don't confuse it with fusilli: While both are called corkscrew pasta, fusilli is a flat twist, whereas cavatappi is a hollow ridged tube that traps thick cheese sauce both inside and out.

Origin: The shape was created by accident in the 1970s at Barilla in Parma, Italy, when pasta dies were mistakenly cut with spiral lines instead of straight ones. Barilla trademarked it as Cellentani after Italian entertainer Adriano Celentano (nicknamed Il Molleggiato, "the springy one"), so other brands adopted the generic name cavatappi.
